# Public EV Charging Landscape in the US

The electric vehicle (EV) landscape in the United States is rapidly evolving, especially with the increasing shift towards electric fleets for companies. As of 2023, the number of public EV charging points has shown significant growth, making it feasible for organizations to transition to electric vehicles. This briefing outlines the current state of public charging infrastructure in the US and highlights two major charging networks that can support this transition.

## Overview of Public Charging Points

As of early 2023, there are approximately 250,000 public EV charging ports across the United States, distributed across over 80,000 charging stations. This marks a substantial increase from earlier years, highlighting a trend toward expanding charging infrastructure to meet the rising demand for electric vehicles. The growth is fueled by both government initiatives and the private sector's commitment to sustainability. Currently, about 1,000 new public chargers are added each week, indicating robust infrastructural development.

## Major Charging Networks

### 1. ChargePoint
ChargePoint is one of the largest and most widely recognized charging networks in the United States. They operate more than 30,000 charging locations and offer a range of charging solutions suitable for both commercial and residential needs. ChargePoint stations are compatible with most electric vehicles, making them a flexible choice for companies transitioning their fleets. Their network provides detailed availability information through their app, enabling users to locate charging stations conveniently.

### 2. Tesla Supercharger
Tesla's Supercharger network is another major player in the public EV charging landscape. With over 1,500 stations and thousands of individual Superchargers across North America, Tesla has established a reliable network for its vehicles. While primarily designed for Tesla vehicles, the company has also begun opening a portion of its stations to non-Tesla EVs, further expanding accessibility. The Supercharger stations are strategically located near major highways and urban areas, ensuring that long-distance travel is more feasible for electric vehicle owners.

In summary, the ongoing expansion of EV charging infrastructure and the presence of robust charging networks, like ChargePoint and Tesla Supercharger, play a crucial role in supporting the electric vehicle adoption among commercial fleets. Companies looking to transition to electric fleets can rely on these charging facilities to maintain their operations effectively.
