# Fleet Comparison: Tesla Model Y vs Ford Mustang Mach-E

## Executive summary
For a 100-car sales fleet, both the Tesla Model Y and Ford Mustang Mach-E are credible electric crossover choices. The Tesla Model Y is the stronger overall fleet efficiency play because of its longer range, fast charging convenience, and lower routine-maintenance burden. The Ford Mustang Mach-E is still a solid option, especially for teams that value Ford dealer support, a familiar brand, and available hands-free highway assistance with BlueCruise.

**Recommended model: Tesla Model Y**

## Tesla Model Y research summary

### Vehicle overview
The Tesla Model Y is a compact/midsize electric SUV with strong volume-seller status and broad market recognition. It is available primarily as a 5-seat vehicle, with some markets or versions offering a 7-seat configuration.

### Practicality
Tesla lists cargo space of about **74 cubic feet** with the rear seats folded, which is strong for a crossover. For sales staff who need to carry sample materials, luggage, or occasional customer items, this is a practical advantage.

### Range and charging
A key strength is range, with current published figures reaching about **321 miles EPA-estimated** depending on trim. Charging is another major advantage: Tesla’s Supercharger network and native charging standard make long-distance and high-utilization fleet operation easier than many non-Tesla EVs.

### Maintenance and service
Tesla states the Model Y does not require annual maintenance or regular fluid changes. That can reduce downtime and simplify fleet management. However, real-world service experience can vary by region, and body repair or specialized service needs may still create delays.

### Safety and driver assistance
Model Y includes features such as Automatic Emergency Braking, Forward Collision Warning, Emergency Lane Departure Avoidance, and Traffic-Aware Cruise Control. Autopilot/FSD options may be available depending on configuration, but these systems require active supervision and are not autonomous.

### Fleet suitability
**Pros:**
- Long range
- Excellent charging ecosystem
- Strong cargo flexibility
- Lower routine maintenance burden
- Strong tech and safety appeal for sales teams

**Cons:**
- Range will drop in cold weather and at sustained highway speeds
- Service and repair experience can be uneven depending on geography
- Pricing and trim availability can change quickly
- Driver-assist systems are not self-driving and need oversight

## Ford Mustang Mach-E research summary

### Vehicle overview
The Ford Mustang Mach-E is an all-electric crossover SUV with 5-seat capacity. It is positioned as a mainstream EV crossover with a more traditional automotive feel and a familiar Ford dealer/service structure.

### Practicality
Ford lists cargo space of about **59.7 cubic feet behind the first row**. It is practical for daily sales use, though not as spacious as the Model Y for cargo-heavy work.

### Range and charging
Published EPA-estimated range varies by trim and configuration, with roughly **240–300 miles** being a reasonable current range band. DC fast charging is supported, and Ford’s BlueOval Charge Network plus home charging options help with day-to-day operations.

### Maintenance and service
Like any EV, routine maintenance is simpler than with a gas vehicle, but the Mach-E still needs tire rotations, cabin filters, brake fluid checks, software updates, and monitoring of charging systems. Ford’s dealer network may be attractive for fleet service coverage.

### Safety and driver assistance
Ford offers Co-Pilot360 features, and BlueCruise is available on equipped trims for hands-free highway driving. That can reduce fatigue on highway-heavy routes, but it remains a driver-assist system rather than full autonomy.

### Fleet suitability
**Pros:**
- Familiar Ford brand and dealer support
- Good daily usability for sales teams
- Available hands-free highway assist on some trims
- Lower routine maintenance than ICE vehicles
- Competitive pricing on entry trims

**Cons:**
- Less cargo space than Model Y
- Range is generally lower than Model Y
- Charging experience depends more heavily on external networks and depot planning
- Higher trims can become expensive quickly

## Fleet decision factors

### 1. Total range and charging convenience
For a sales fleet, time is money. Vehicles that can handle longer daily routes and recharge quickly are less disruptive. The Model Y has the advantage here because of its range and access to Tesla Superchargers.

### 2. Cargo and passenger practicality
Both vehicles seat five, but the Model Y offers more cargo space. If representatives often carry demonstration items or travel gear, that extra room matters.

### 3. Service and downtime
The Mustang Mach-E may appeal to fleets that prefer Ford dealer service access. The Model Y benefits from simpler routine maintenance, but service experiences can vary by market.

### 4. Driver-assist value
Both offer useful driver-assist features. BlueCruise may be attractive for highway-heavy reps, while Tesla’s suite is well known and widely used. In both cases, these systems do not replace driver attention.

### 5. Cost
The Mach-E can start at a lower MSRP, but final fleet pricing depends heavily on trim and incentives. The Model Y often offsets higher sticker price with better charging convenience and range, which can reduce operational friction.

---

## Final recommendation
For a **100-car sales fleet**, the **Tesla Model Y** is the better overall choice.

### Why
- Better range for real-world sales driving
- Stronger charging ecosystem for fleet utilization
- More cargo space
- Lower routine maintenance burden
- Excellent fit for reps who need reliability and minimal downtime between calls

### When the Mach-E would be the better choice
Choose the **Ford Mustang Mach-E** if your organization prioritizes:
- Ford dealer service relationships
- BlueCruise for highway-heavy routes
- Potentially lower upfront trim pricing
- Brand familiarity for a more traditional fleet setup

**Bottom line:** If the fleet’s top priority is operational efficiency and charging convenience, go with the **Tesla Model Y**.
